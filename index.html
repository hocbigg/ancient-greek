Introduction

Studying Ancient Greek provides direct, unmediated access to the foundational texts of Western literature, philosophy, drama, and historiography. Rather than relying on modern translations that inevitably smooth over ideological tensions, rhetorical nuances, and poetic meters, learning the language allows you to encounter authors like Homer, Plato, and Euripides on their own terms.

This curriculum is designed for absolute beginners and assumes no prior background in classical philology, Latin, or linguistics. It establishes a complete foundational baseline, taking you from the Greek alphabet and basic noun-verb morphology to reading unadapted classical prose and verse. Its scope is deliberately focused on the two primary literary dialects of the classical period: Attic Greek, the literary standard of fifth- and fourth-century BCE Athens, and Homeric Greek, the archaic artificial dialect of epic poetry. Post-classical developments (such as Hellenistic Koine, the Second Sophistic, and Byzantine Greek) and auxiliary material disciplines are reserved for subsequent study.

Because classical language acquisition is strictly cumulative, you should work through the subjects in a clear linear progression:

  • Grammatical Foundation: Begin with Foundations of Attic Greek. Mastering nominal declensions, the principal parts of the verb system, and foundational syntax is mandatory before attempting continuous ancient texts.
  • Prose Fluency and Active Syntax: Transition directly into Intermediate Attic Prose and Greek Syntax and Prose Composition. Reading unadapted historical narrative and courtroom oratory (Xenophon, Lysias) builds reading stamina, while active prose composition reinforces complex clause structures (such as conditional sentences and indirect discourse) from the inside out.
  • Philosophical Prose: Advance to Classical Philosophy: Plato to develop confidence with philosophical dialogue, conversational idiom, and sustained dialectical argument.
  • Poetic Dialects and Meters: Tackle verse only after establishing solid prose fluency. Homeric Epic Poetry introduces archaic epic morphology and dactylic hexameter, while Attic Drama and Tragedy transitions into fifth-century tragic diction, iambic trimeter, and choral lyric.
  • Historical Framework: Greek Historical and Cultural Context should be studied alongside your intermediate coursework or immediately following it, providing the civic and historical grounding required to interpret these texts in their original environments.

Once you have completed this core sequence and achieved reading fluency in Attic and epic texts, you can branch out into specialized subfields in Advanced Topics, including historical linguistics, epigraphy, papyrology, and post-classical Greek. To explore landmark secondary scholarship on Greek religion, oral-formulaic theory, and textual criticism, consult Readings; for complementary university lecture series and open audio deep-dives, see Courses.

Curriculum

Foundations of Attic Greek

Covers the nominal declensions, verbal conjugations, core vocabulary, and foundational syntax of classical Attic Greek.

Introduction to Attic Greek by Donald J. Mastronarde (University of California Press) - The primary foundational textbook, offering systematic explanations of Attic morphology, accentuation, and basic sentence structure.

Ancient Greek Tutorials (Donald J. Mastronarde / UC Berkeley) - An open-access interactive companion designed to reinforce Mastronarde's textbook through audio pronunciation guides, accent tutorials, and morphological drills.

Learn Ancient Greek (Harvard Center for Hellenic Studies / Prof. Leonard Muellner) - A complete 114-lecture open video course delivering two semesters of university grammar instruction, closely coordinated with Hansen and Quinn.

Greek: An Intensive Course by Hardy Hansen and Gerald M. Quinn (Fordham University Press) - A fast-paced, drill-intensive alternative to Mastronarde that pairs directly with Professor Muellner’s video lectures.

Intermediate Attic Prose

Covers continuous, unadapted classical Attic narrative and forensic oratory to transition students from isolated textbook sentences to genuine ancient literature.

Xenophon's Anabasis I: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - An open-access guided reader featuring running vocabulary and syntax notes on facing pages, serving as the standard initial bridge to continuous prose.

Lysias I: On the Murder of Eratosthenes: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - A sequential reader to study after Xenophon, introducing standard Attic courtroom rhetoric and idiomatic conversational syntax.

Greek Syntax and Prose Composition

Covers advanced Attic sentence structure, mood usage (subjunctive and optative), and active composition from English into Greek to solidify grammatical mastery.

An Introduction to the Composition and Analysis of Greek Prose by Eleanor Dickey (Cambridge University Press) - Canonical modern textbook for mastering complex Attic syntax through linguistic explanations, clause analysis, and active composition exercises.

Greek Prose Composition by Henry Carr Pearson (American Book Company / Internet Archive) - A public-domain alternative and supplementary drill book focused on sentence-level translation exercises keyed to Xenophon's vocabulary.

Classical Philosophy: Plato

Covers Socratic dialogue, dialectical argumentation, and the literary style of middle-period Platonic prose.

Plato's Apology: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - The primary entry text for Platonic Greek, supported by facing vocabulary and grammatical notes to ease the transition into philosophical dialogue.

Plato's Crito: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - A direct sequel to the Apology that reinforces Platonic ethical argumentation and conversational syntax at the intermediate level.

Plato: Symposium, edited by Kenneth Dover (Cambridge University Press) - A scholarly commentary to tackle after Steadman's readers, providing advanced philological and literary analysis of unadapted Platonic prose.

PLSC 114: Introduction to Political Philosophy (Open Yale Courses) - A free lecture series whose opening lectures provide contextual background on the historical trial of Socrates and Plato's political philosophy.

Homeric Epic Poetry

Covers the Homeric dialect, archaic epic morphology, and dactylic hexameter meter through Homer's Iliad and Odyssey.

Homeric Greek: A Book for Beginners by Clyde Pharr, revised by John Wright and Paula Debnar (University of Oklahoma Press) - The foundational transitional textbook that systematically introduces the grammar, dialectical forms, and meter of epic poetry using Iliad Book 1.

Homer's Iliad 6 & 22: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - Open-access guided reader designed to follow Pharr's textbook, providing running vocabulary and metrical aids for two major books of the Iliad.

Homer's Odyssey 6–8: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - A complementary guided reader to be studied alongside or after the Iliad selections, focusing on epic narrative style in the Odyssey.

Attic Drama and Tragedy

Covers the poetic syntax, tragic vocabulary, and dramatic meters (iambic trimeter and choral lyric) of classical Athenian tragedy.

Euripides' Medea: Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary by Geoffrey Steadman - An open-access guided reader providing facing vocabulary and parsing aids, serving as the gentlest initial entry into reading dramatic verse.

Euripides: Medea, edited by Donald J. Mastronarde (Cambridge University Press) - An advanced commentary to use alongside or after Steadman, notable for its dedicated introductory treatise on tragic language, style, and meters.

Greek Historical and Cultural Context

Covers the historical, political, and literary development of archaic and classical Greece to provide the interpretive framework necessary for reading ancient literature.

CLCV 205: Introduction to Ancient Greek History by Donald Kagan (Open Yale Courses) - A comprehensive 24-lecture university course detailing the development of the Greek polis, the Persian and Peloponnesian Wars, and Athenian democracy.

Ancient Greece: From Prehistoric to Hellenistic Times by Thomas R. Martin (Yale University Press) - A concise, accessible narrative history that serves as the primary companion reading for Donald Kagan’s lecture series.

The Cambridge History of Classical Literature: Volume 1, Greek Literature, edited by P. E. Easterling and B. M. W. Knox (Cambridge University Press) - The standard historical reference survey covering the cultural context, genres, and authors of Greek literature from Homer to the classical period.
